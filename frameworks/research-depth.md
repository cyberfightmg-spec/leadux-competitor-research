# Research Depth Framework v0.2

Research depth controls breadth, number of research waves, source diversity and validation effort. It never relaxes the evidence rules.

## Quick

Use for:

- reconnaissance;
- a narrow competitor comparison;
- fast pricing check;
- early market scan.

Typical behavior:

- one or two search waves;
- focus on 3–5 highly relevant competitors when available;
- primary sources first;
- limited VOC/GTM depth;
- explicit data gaps;
- verification of any claim that materially changes the answer.

Quick mode may be incomplete, but must never invent missing data.

## Standard

Use for:

- normal competitor intelligence;
- positioning/product/pricing comparisons;
- moderate business decisions;
- market maps with actionable findings.

Typical behavior:

- multiple query families;
- breadth discovery plus entity verification;
- roughly 5–10 Tier A competitors where the market supports it;
- selected Tier B context;
- multiple source families;
- customer/VOC evidence where relevant;
- contradiction check for important findings;
- targeted verification.

The competitor count is guidance, not a quota. A niche may legitimately contain fewer relevant entities.

## Deep

Use for:

- market entry;
- product strategy;
- serious pricing/positioning decisions;
- opportunity/whitespace validation;
- high-stakes executive research;
- explicit “deep/full” requests.

Required behavior:

1. scope/category challenge;
2. capability detection;
3. geographic source-pack selection;
4. discovery breadth wave;
5. entity verification;
6. tiering;
7. deep profiles for Tier A;
8. product/pricing/customer/VOC/GTM/signals as relevant;
9. gap-closure search wave;
10. contradiction check;
11. evidence verification;
12. red team;
13. final synthesis with data gaps.

Deep mode should search until decision-relevant saturation rather than until a fixed source count is reached.

## Dynamic allocation

Do not distribute effort equally.

Allocate more depth to:

- competitors with highest JTBD/ICP overlap;
- competitors driving the recommendation;
- uncertain high-impact claims;
- contradictions;
- market gaps that appear strategically attractive;
- source areas where one additional check can materially change confidence.

Allocate less depth to:

- duplicate/low-relevance competitors;
- copied secondary sources;
- low-impact descriptive facts;
- dimensions irrelevant to the research decision.

## Gap-closure rule

After the first synthesis draft, identify the top unanswered questions that could change the decision.

Run a targeted second pass only for those gaps.

Do not continue broad crawling merely to increase source count.

## Saturation

Research can stop when:

- important competitor types are represented;
- two search waves add few/no new high-relevance entities;
- high-impact questions are evidenced or explicitly unresolved;
- remaining new sources are mostly duplicates/low-value;
- additional uncertainty is unlikely to change the decision without primary research.

## Degraded research

If tools or source families fail:

- continue with fallbacks;
- mark `status = degraded` where material;
- list unavailable source families;
- lower confidence only for affected claims;
- never represent tool failure as a negative market signal.

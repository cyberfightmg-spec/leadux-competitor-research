# Source Quality Framework v0.2

Source quality has multiple dimensions. Do not reduce all of them to one authority score.

## 1. Source tier

### Tier A — official / primary public record

Examples:

- government statistics;
- official registries;
- regulatory filings;
- court/procurement records;
- original datasets.

Best for facts within their actual scope.

### Tier B — company primary source

Examples:

- official pricing;
- documentation;
- product pages;
- changelog;
- public offer/terms;
- official announcements;
- official careers pages.

Best for what the company publicly offers/claims/changes.

Company sources are not independent validation of outcomes.

### Tier C — reputable specialist / research / industry source

Examples:

- established industry associations;
- credible research organizations;
- specialist media with transparent sourcing;
- original surveys/reports.

Evaluate methodology and underlying source.

### Tier D — customer/community/platform signal

Examples:

- reviews;
- forums;
- public community discussions;
- app-store feedback;
- map reviews;
- public comments.

Strong for VOC and lived experience, weaker for market totals or corporate financial facts.

### Tier E — aggregator / estimate / secondary summary

Use for discovery, triangulation and explicitly labeled estimates.

Trace back to the original source when possible.

### Tier F — unknown / unverifiable

Do not use as sole support for material claims.

---

# 2. Separate quality dimensions

For each source/claim consider:

## Directness

Does the source directly establish the claim or merely mention it?

## Reliability

Is the source appropriate and credible for this type of fact?

## Relevance

Does it refer to the same entity, geography, product, segment and metric?

## Freshness

Is it current enough for the claim?

## Independence

Is it independent of other cited sources, or are they all repeating one origin?

## Method transparency

For estimates/reports, can the methodology be inspected?

---

# 3. Source selection by claim type

## Current competitor price

Prefer:

1. official pricing/billing source;
2. public offer/terms;
3. official marketplace/app listing;
4. reputable secondary source as fallback.

## Legal/company identity

Prefer official registry / legal documents.

## Customer pain

Prefer multiple independent customer/community/review sources and preserve sample bias notes.

## Market size

Prefer official statistics or transparent original research; ensure market definition matches.

## Product feature

Prefer current docs/product help pages; reviews may validate experience but not canonical availability.

## Strategic change

Prefer dated official announcement/changelog/registry source, then corroborate where useful.

---

# 4. Primary source does not always mean unbiased truth

Examples:

- official pricing page is strong evidence of listed pricing;
- homepage promise is strong evidence of positioning;
- homepage claim “cuts costs by 80%” is not independent evidence the outcome occurs;
- customer testimonial hosted by the vendor is not independent VOC.

Match source type to claim type.

---

# 5. Independence groups

Assign related sources an origin family when practical.

Example:

```text
Company press release
→ news article A
→ news article B
→ aggregator C
```

This is one originating claim family, not four independent confirmations.

---

# 6. Access limitations

Use:

```text
ok
partial
protected
unavailable
```

A protected/unavailable source should reduce coverage where material but must never be converted into a negative market fact.

---

# 7. Confidence guidance

### High

Direct, relevant, current evidence; corroborated where the claim warrants it; no unresolved material contradiction.

### Medium

Useful evidence but one dimension is weaker: indirect source, limited sample, moderate staleness, or incomplete corroboration.

### Low

Single weak/indirect source, uncertain scope, stale evidence, or material unresolved contradiction.

Confidence should be assigned to claims/insights, not vaguely to an entire market.

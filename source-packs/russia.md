# Russia Source Pack

Use this source pack when the research geography includes the Russian Federation.

The goal is not to force every source into every project. Select source families according to the research questions and market type.

## Source hierarchy

### Tier A — official / primary public data

Prefer for legal status, company identity, official statistics, public procurement and regulation.

#### Federal Tax Service (ФНС)

- https://www.nalog.gov.ru/
- Open taxpayer/company services: https://www.nalog.gov.ru/donline/

Useful for publicly available company and entrepreneur information, status, extracts, OKVED and related official data exposed by FNS services.

Do not infer revenue, customer count or operational performance from registration data alone.

#### Rosstat / official statistics

- https://rosstat.gov.ru/
- Statistics: https://rosstat.gov.ru/statistic
- Databases: https://rosstat.gov.ru/databases

Useful for market context, population, industries, employment, prices, production, regional and municipal statistics where available.

Always state the statistical period and unit. Do not present national statistics as a direct measurement of a narrow niche unless the mapping is justified.

#### Unified Information System in Procurement (ЕИС)

- https://zakupki.gov.ru/

Use public procurement records when relevant to B2G demand, supplier activity, contract categories and public-sector buying signals.

A procurement record proves a specific procurement event; it does not by itself prove total market demand.

#### Fedresurs

- https://fedresurs.ru/

Use publicly accessible disclosures when relevant to company events and legally published notices.

Verify the entity carefully by INN/OGRN or other identifiers where possible; names alone may be ambiguous.

#### Other official sources by domain

Depending on the niche, consider the relevant regulator or official registry, for example:

- Bank of Russia — https://cbr.ru/
- Rospatent / FIPS — https://rospatent.gov.ru/ and https://fips.ru/
- official government/regulator portals relevant to the industry.

Use only when the source directly answers a research question.

## Tier B — company primary sources

Use official competitor sources for claims about the competitor's current public offer:

- official website;
- pricing pages;
- product documentation;
- terms and public offer documents;
- changelog/release notes;
- official Telegram/VK/YouTube channels;
- official app/store listings;
- official case studies and customer pages;
- official vacancies/career pages.

Marketing claims are evidence of **what the company claims**, not independent proof that the claim is true.

---

## Tier C — demand, customer and distribution signals

### Yandex Maps

- https://yandex.ru/maps/

Useful for local-business discovery, category density, public ratings/reviews, locations, operating information and customer language.

Treat reviews as VOC/community evidence. Ratings are platform signals, not a universal measure of business quality.

### 2GIS

- https://2gis.ru/

Useful for local company discovery, locations, categories and public business-card/review signals where available.

Respect platform access rules and do not circumvent restrictions.

### hh.ru

- https://hh.ru/

Public vacancies may provide hiring/role signals.

A vacancy is evidence that a role is being advertised; it is not sufficient proof of growth, profitability or successful expansion.

### Marketplaces and app stores

Depending on the niche, public listings and reviews on relevant marketplaces or app stores may provide:

- price observations;
- assortment;
- review language;
- seller/product presence;
- feature complaints.

Always distinguish platform seller data from manufacturer/company data.

---

## Tier D — community / public discussion

Potential sources include publicly accessible:

- Telegram channels/posts;
- VK communities;
- YouTube videos/comments;
- forums;
- review websites;
- professional communities.

Use mainly for:

- VOC;
- pains;
- objections;
- switching reasons;
- alternative workflows;
- emerging language/trends.

Do not convert a small discussion sample into a market-size or purchasing-demand claim.

---

## Tier E — media / analyst / secondary sources

Use reputable business/industry media and specialist publications for context, events and attributed estimates.

Whenever a secondary article cites an original report, filing, company statement or government statistic, prefer and preserve the original source.

Do not treat multiple articles based on the same press release as independent corroboration.

# Russian-language search expansion

For Russian markets, generate variants across:

- singular/plural;
- common case forms;
- abbreviations;
- colloquial buyer language;
- English product/category names;
- transliterated brand names;
- city/region terms when geography is local.

Examples:

```text
CRM автосервис
CRM для автосервиса
программа автосервиса
учёт в автосервисе
автоматизация СТО
система для СТО
запись клиентов автосервис
```

Do not rely only on the formal industry category; buyers may describe the same job differently.

# Russia-specific entity resolution

When researching organizations, prefer stable identifiers where publicly available:

```text
INN
OGRN / OGRNIP
official domain
legal entity name
brand name
region
```

Do not merge two businesses solely because their brand names are similar.

# Russia-specific research integrity

- Prices must include observation date and VAT/tax context when known and relevant.
- Public tender amounts are procurement facts, not competitor revenue.
- Registered OKVED is an official classification signal, not proof that the company currently earns most revenue from that activity.
- Vacancy count is a hiring signal, not employee count.
- Review counts/ratings are platform-specific signals.
- Telegram subscriber count is audience size on that channel, not customer count.
